# Vegan — Decision Rules for Meal Planning

Load this doc when the user is vegan. This covers everything beyond vegetarian.md — the additional challenges of having NO eggs or dairy.

**Key finding:** A well-planned vegan diet can support athletic performance (Hevia-Larrain et al. 2021: vegans and omnivores both at 1.6g/kg gained equivalent lean mass over 12 weeks). But "well-planned" means more supplementation and more deliberate food choices than any other dietary pattern.

## Protein Strategy

Vegan protein requires the most planning of any diet. Key challenges:
- Most plant proteins are incomplete (low in one or more essential amino acids)
- Plant proteins have lower leucine content — need larger portions to trigger MPS
- Digestibility is lower (DIAAS: soy ~0.90, legumes ~0.62-0.76, grains ~0.40-0.60)

### Protein Target Adjustment
Add ~10-20% to standard protein targets. This is based on digestibility data rather than direct RCTs, but it's a reasonable insurance policy.
- Cutting: 2.2-2.6 g/kg (vs 2.0-2.4 for omnivores)
- Bulking: 1.8-2.4 g/kg (vs 1.6-2.2)
- If strategic about sources (soy, pea+rice blends), the lower end is fine

### Leucine Threshold (~2.5-3g per meal)

| Source | Leucine per serve | Amount needed for 3g |
|--------|------------------|---------------------|
| Soy protein isolate (30g scoop) | ~2.5g | 36g |
| Pea protein isolate (30g scoop) | ~2.0g | 45g |
| Seitan (100g) | ~2.2-2.7g | ~115g |
| Tempeh (150g) | ~2.5g | ~175g for 3g |
| Tofu firm (150g) | ~2.5g | ~175g for 3g |
| Lentils (1 cup cooked) | ~1.6g | ~2 cups (impractical) |
| Chickpeas (1 cup cooked) | ~1.0g | ~3 cups (impractical) |

**Rule:** Hitting leucine from whole-food vegan sources alone is hard. Use soy/pea protein powder to top up meals, or combine seitan + legumes. Legume-only meals will not hit the threshold at practical portion sizes.

### Protein Combining
- **Same meal NOT required** — the body maintains a free amino acid pool and recycles ~90g protein daily from endogenous turnover. American Dietetic Association reversed their position on this in 1988.
- **But for MPS:** A single plant meal shows ~47% lower acute postprandial MPS than a meat meal. The gap closes over 24 hours with adequate total intake.
- **Practical rule:** Eat a variety across the day. Ensure daily intake includes both lysine-rich (legumes, soy) and methionine-rich (grains, seeds, nuts) sources.

### Soy Safety
Soy does NOT affect male hormones at normal dietary intake. Confirmed by multiple meta-analyses:
- Hamilton-Reeves et al. 2010: No effects on testosterone, SHBG, or free testosterone
- Reed et al. 2021 (38 clinical studies): Same conclusion
- Rajaie et al. 2025 (dose-response): No effects from 10-70g soy protein daily

**Rule:** Soy is safe. Eat it freely. 2-4 servings daily is well within safe limits. The phytoestrogen concern is not supported by clinical evidence.

## Mandatory Supplements

These are non-negotiable — cannot be adequately obtained from a vegan diet:

| Supplement | Dose | Why |
|------------|------|-----|
| **Vitamin B12** | 250-500mcg/day or 2500mcg/week | Zero reliable dietary sources. Deficiency causes irreversible nerve damage. |
| **Iodine (potassium iodide)** | 150mcg/day | Vegan median iodine is ~1/6th of omnivore levels (UK study). No dairy = no iodine buffer. Do NOT use kelp (wildly variable content — 33 to 7,800mcg/g). |
| **Vitamin D3 (lichen-derived)** | 2000-4000 IU/day | Use D3 not D2 — D3 raises serum levels ~75% more than D2 (meta-analysis). Lichen D3 is molecularly identical to animal D3. |
| **Omega-3 (algae DHA/EPA)** | 2-3g combined/day | No fish, no eggs. ALA conversion to DHA is <1%. Algae is the source fish get it from. |
| **Creatine monohydrate** | 5g/day | Zero dietary creatine. Benefits are larger than for omnivores. |

### Strongly Recommended
| Supplement | Dose | Why |
|------------|------|-----|
| **Beta-alanine** | 3-6g/day | Zero dietary carnosine. Vegetarians have 26-50% less muscle carnosine. Rogerson 2017 specifically recommends this for vegan athletes. |
| **Calcium citrate** | 500mg/day | Only if not using fortified plant milk daily and eating calcium-rich greens |

### Conditional
| Supplement | Dose | Notes |
|------------|------|-------|
| Zinc | 15-30mg | If relying heavily on legumes/grains. Soak/sprout to improve absorption. |
| Iron | Test first | Non-heme only. Never supplement without blood work. |
| Taurine | 500-1000mg | Lower plasma levels in vegans (22%). Unclear performance impact. |

## Critical Nutrition Rules

### Iron
Only non-heme iron — absorbs at 2-20% vs heme iron's 15-35%.
- **Always pair with vitamin C** at the same meal. Make this automatic in every recipe with lentils/beans/tofu.
- **Avoid tea/coffee** within 1 hour of iron-rich meals
- **Best sources:** Lentils, tofu, tempeh, fortified cereals, pumpkin seeds
- **Cook in cast iron** when practical

### Calcium Without Dairy
Target 1000mg/day. Difficult without fortified foods.

| Source | Absorption | Notes |
|--------|-----------|-------|
| Broccoli | 61% | Excellent — better absorbed than dairy |
| Bok choy | 54% | Excellent |
| Kale | ~50% | Excellent |
| Calcium-set tofu | Moderate | ~350mg/100g — good source |
| Fortified plant milk | ~30% (like dairy) | 120mg/100ml. **Shake the carton — calcium settles.** |
| Spinach, chard | 5% | **Useless for calcium.** Oxalate blocks absorption. |

**Rule:** Use calcium-fortified plant milk daily. Include calcium-set tofu regularly. Specify "fortified" in shopping lists. Boiling high-oxalate veg reduces oxalate by 30-87%.

### Zinc
Phytate in legumes/grains reduces bioavailability. Same mitigation as vegetarian doc:
- Soak dried legumes (38-44% improvement)
- Use fermented soy (tempeh, miso) over unfermented — fermentation reduces phytate by up to 81%
- Leavened bread > unleavened

## Meal Planning Rules for Vegans

1. **Every meal must have a deliberate protein source** — not just "there's some protein in the rice"
2. Soy-based protein (tofu, tempeh, edamame) at least once daily — it's the easiest complete source
3. Include vitamin C with every iron-rich meal (make this automatic)
4. Use calcium-fortified plant milk — specify "fortified" in shopping lists
5. Batch-cook legumes weekly (lentils, chickpeas, black beans) — they're the foundation
6. Nutritional yeast as a regular seasoning (B12 if fortified + protein + umami)
7. Seitan is protein-dense but lysine-deficient — always pair with legumes in the same meal or day
8. Higher protein targets than omnivore recommendations (add 10-20%)
9. Check sauces, bread, pasta for hidden animal products when writing shopping lists
10. Pea+rice protein blend in shakes (complementary amino acids, approaches animal protein quality)

## Common Pitfalls
- Protein too low because "I had hummus and rice" counts as a meal but has ~15g protein
- Relying on processed vegan products (beyond burger, vegan cheese) — often UPFs with poor nutrition
- Not supplementing B12 ("I eat nutritional yeast" — usually not enough on its own unless fortified and eaten in large quantities)
- Ignoring iodine entirely — the most under-discussed serious vegan deficiency risk
- Not getting enough calcium because plant milk isn't fortified or isn't shaken
- Too many nuts/seeds for protein — calorie-dense, easy to overshoot on a cut
- Using D2 instead of lichen D3 — D2 is roughly half as effective
- Counting spinach as an iron or calcium source
