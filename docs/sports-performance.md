# Sports Performance — Decision Rules for Meal Planning

Load this doc when the user has training/body composition goals.

## Protein Targets by Goal

| Goal | Protein (g/kg bodyweight) | Notes |
|------|--------------------------|-------|
| **Cutting** | 2.0-2.4 | Higher end preserves muscle. Longland 2016: 2.4g/kg gained lean mass during deficit while 1.2g/kg lost it. |
| **Maintenance** | 1.6-2.0 | Standard athletic range |
| **Bulking** | 1.6-2.2 | Diminishing returns above 2.2. Extra calories should come from carbs, not more protein. |
| **Recomp** | 2.0-2.4 | Similar to cutting — slight deficit or maintenance calories with high protein |

**Distribution:**
- 4-5 meals with 30-50g protein each
- Leucine threshold: ~2.5-3g per meal for muscle protein synthesis
- Casein or slow protein before bed supports overnight recovery
- Total daily intake matters more than precise timing — the "anabolic window" is largely debunked

## Protein Sources — Ranked for Athletes

| Source | Per 100g (approx) | Key Benefits |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|
| Chicken breast | 165 kcal, 31g protein, 3.6g fat | Leanest option |
| Lean beef mince (5%) | 137 kcal, 21g protein, 6g fat | Iron, B12, zinc, creatine. Good fat profile. |
| Regular beef mince (15-20%) | 230 kcal, 18g protein, 17g fat | Same micronutrients. Prefer this over lean + added fat. |
| Salmon | 208 kcal, 20g protein, 13g fat | Omega-3s. Include 2x/week minimum. |
| Eggs | 155 kcal, 13g protein, 11g fat | Complete protein + choline. |
| Greek yogurt | 59 kcal, 10g protein, 0.7g fat | Protein + probiotics |
| Whey protein | ~120 kcal/30g scoop, 24g protein | Fast absorption, high leucine. Best post-workout. |

**Rule: Rotate protein sources across the week.** Don't default to chicken for everything — beef provides iron/zinc/creatine, fish provides omega-3s, eggs provide choline.

## Cutting-Specific Rules

- Prioritise high-satiety foods: potatoes, fish, oats, eggs, legumes
- Maintain fat minimum: 0.8-1.0 g/kg bodyweight. Below this, testosterone drops 10-15%.
- Keep carbs adequate for training quality — don't go low-carb
- Rate of loss: aim for 0.25-0.5 kg/week to preserve muscle. Faster = more muscle loss.
- If energy drops, check carb intake first
- Poor sleep during a cut shifts weight loss toward muscle — prioritise sleep

## Bulking-Specific Rules

**Key finding: larger surpluses mostly add fat, not muscle.** Helms et al. (2023) RCT compared 5% vs 15% surplus in trained lifters — faster weight gain primarily increased fat accumulation, not muscle thickness or strength.

- **Surplus:** 200-300 kcal/day (~5-10%) for trained lifters. Novices can tolerate slightly more. (Iraki et al. 2019)
- **Rate of gain:** 0.25-0.5% bodyweight/week. For an 84kg lifter that's ~0.2-0.4 kg/week. Faster = mostly fat. (Iraki 2019, Helms 2023)
- **Protein:** 1.6-2.2 g/kg is sufficient in surplus — the surplus itself is anti-catabolic. Gains plateau at ~1.6 g/kg (Morton et al. 2018 meta-analysis, n=1,863). No benefit to going above 2.2.
- **Carbs:** Fill remaining calories after protein and fat. Target ≥3-5 g/kg/day. Carb-heavy surpluses produce better muscle:fat ratios than fat-heavy surpluses (Lambert et al. 2004).
- **Fat:** Minimum 0.7-1.0 g/kg for hormonal health, but don't overshoot — excess fat surplus partitions worse than excess carb surplus.
- **Rule:** Extra calories from a bulk should come from carbs, not protein or fat. If someone is bulking and asks for "more food," add rice/potatoes/oats, not extra meat or cheese.

## Recomp Rules

**Recomp is real but slower than bulk/cut cycles.** Works best for specific populations.

- **Who it works for:** Beginners, returning from a break, higher body fat (>15%), anyone willing to accept a slower timeline. (Barakat et al. 2020)
- **Evidence:** Longland et al. (2016) showed simultaneous muscle gain + fat loss even in a 40% deficit with 2.4 g/kg protein + high-intensity exercise.
- **Calories:** Maintenance or very slight deficit (-100 to -200 kcal)
- **Protein:** 2.0-2.4 g/kg — this is the critical variable. High protein + resistance training is what drives recomp.
- **Progress tracking:** Use body measurements, photos, or DEXA — not just the scale. Weight may stay flat while body composition changes.
- **Rule:** For lean, trained individuals, traditional bulk/cut is more efficient. Suggest recomp only when the user's profile matches the "works for" criteria above.

## Carbohydrates for Performance

**Target: 2-4 g/kg bodyweight**, scaled to training intensity.

Evidence does NOT support very low-carb for optimal athletic performance. Adequate carbs:
- Maintain training quality
- Support glycogen replenishment
- Aid sleep quality (moderate GI evening meal)

**Timing:**
- Around training: prioritise carbs pre/post for energy and recovery
- Evening: moderate carb meal supports sleep

**Best sources:**
| Source | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Potatoes (white and sweet) | Cook and cool for resistant starch. Highest satiety. |
| Rice | White for digestibility. Include some whole grains for variety. |
| Oats | Beta-glucan for gut health. Good breakfast base. |
| Fruits | Especially berries (fibre + polyphenols) |
| Root vegetables | Carrots, parsnips, beets |

## Fats — Minimum Thresholds

**Target: 0.8-1.0 g/kg bodyweight minimum** (even when cutting)

Priorities:
1. **Omega-3s (EPA/DHA):** 2-3g combined daily from fish or supplements. Recovery, inflammation, brain.
2. **Saturated fat:** Needed for hormones. Butter, ghee, animal fats — don't fear them.
3. **Monounsaturated:** Olive oil, avocado — heart health and satiety.
4. **Minimise:** Omega-6 from seed oils.

**Rule:** If a meal needs more fat, prefer upgrading the protein source (lean → regular mince) over adding a separate fat (sour cream, cheese). Same calories, more micronutrients.

## Supplements for Athletes

| Supplement | Dose | Why |
|------------|------|-----|
| Creatine monohydrate | 5g daily | Most researched sports supplement. Strength, power, cognitive function. |
| Vitamin D3 | 2000-4000 IU | Muscle function, immunity, bone density |
| Omega-3 | 2-3g EPA/DHA | Recovery, inflammation, brain structure |
| Magnesium glycinate | 300-400mg before bed | Sleep, muscle relaxation, 300+ enzymes |
| Electrolytes | As needed | Critical when sweating. Salt, potassium, magnesium. |
