# Gut Microbiome — Decision Rules for Meal Planning

Load this doc when the user has gut health goals.

## Core Principles

The Stanford FeFiFo Study (Cell, 2021) is the gold standard: a 17-week RCT found that a high-fermented-food diet steadily increased microbiome diversity AND reduced 19 inflammatory markers. Notably, high fibre alone did NOT increase diversity in the short term — fermented foods did.

## 30+ Different Plants Per Week

From the American Gut Project (10,000+ participants): people eating 30+ plants weekly had significantly more diverse microbiomes and higher levels of beneficial SCFAs.

**What counts as a "plant":**
- Vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, nuts, seeds, herbs, spices
- Each variety counts separately (red pepper and green pepper = 2)

**Rule:** When planning meals, actively vary the plant ingredients across the week. Don't use the same 5 vegetables in every recipe. Track the count if possible.

## Fermented Foods Daily

The Stanford study showed larger servings = stronger effects. Aim for 2-3 servings daily:

| Source | Notes |
|--------|-------|
| Kefir | ~27 billion CFU/cup — strongest evidence |
| Kimchi | Must be unpasteurised (refrigerated section) |
| Sauerkraut | Must be unpasteurised |
| Greek/natural yogurt | Must have live cultures |
| Miso | Use in dressings or soups |
| Tempeh | Fermented soy — also a good protein source |

**Rule:** Add fermented foods AFTER reheating to preserve live cultures. Don't cook kimchi into a dish if gut health is the goal — serve it alongside.

Note: Fermented food microbes may not permanently colonise, but they transiently boost diversity and barrier function. Up to 3% of adult gut microbiota may come from food-derived microbes.

## Resistant Starch

Cook and cool starchy carbs (rice, potatoes, oats). Cooling causes retrogradation — starch molecules realign into a form that resists digestion and feeds beneficial bacteria.

Evidence: RS supplementation for 8 weeks achieved weight loss (mean -2.8 kg) and improved insulin resistance, associated with increases in Bifidobacterium adolescentis (Nature Metabolism, 2024).

**Rule:** Batch cook rice and potatoes, refrigerate overnight, reheat gently. This should be the default for meal prep — it's free resistant starch with no extra effort.

## Prebiotic Fibres

Different fibres feed different bacteria. Include variety across the week:

| Type | Sources |
|------|---------|
| Inulin/FOS | Onions, garlic, leeks, asparagus, bananas |
| Resistant starch | Cooled rice/potatoes, green bananas |
| Beta-glucan | Oats, barley, mushrooms |
| Pectin | Apples, citrus, berries |

**Rule:** Don't avoid "gassy" vegetables (broccoli, cauliflower, onions, garlic) — these are prebiotic powerhouses. If the user isn't used to high fibre, introduce gradually over 2-3 weeks rather than eliminating.

## Foods That Harm the Microbiome

- **UPFs:** Decrease diversity, increase inflammation (see general-health.md)
- **Artificial sweeteners:** Sucralose and acesulfame K are particularly harmful
- **Industrial seed oils:** Pro-inflammatory, associated with reduced diversity
- **Excessive alcohol:** Disrupts gut barrier function

## Meal Planning Rules for Gut Health

1. Every batch meal should include 2+ different plant varieties
2. Fermented food with at least one meal daily (serve on the side, not cooked in)
3. Use the cook-cool-reheat method for starchy carbs by default
4. Rotate vegetables across the week — don't repeat the same ones in every meal
5. Include at least one prebiotic-rich ingredient per meal (onion, garlic, leeks count)
6. If introducing high-fibre foods to someone not used to them, start with smaller portions and increase over 2-3 weeks
