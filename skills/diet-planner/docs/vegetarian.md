# Vegetarian — Decision Rules for Meal Planning

Load this doc when the user is vegetarian (lacto-ovo or similar). For vegan users, load vegan.md instead.

**Key finding:** A well-planned vegetarian diet does not impair athletic performance (Craddock et al. 2016, systematic review of 8 studies). The challenge is specific nutrient gaps, not overall adequacy.

## Protein Strategy

Eggs and dairy make vegetarian protein quality a non-issue — both score ~1.0 PDCAAS, equivalent to meat. The challenge is hitting total protein targets without excess calories, especially during a cut.

### Leucine Threshold (~2.5-3g per meal)

| Source | Leucine per serve | Hits threshold? |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Whey protein (30g scoop) | ~2.5g | Yes |
| 3 large eggs | ~1.6g | Needs pairing |
| Greek yogurt (200g) | ~1.5g | Needs pairing |
| Cottage cheese (200g) | ~2.0g | Close — add eggs or dairy |
| Tofu/tempeh (150g) | ~2.5g | Yes (at a decent portion) |
| Lentils (1 cup cooked) | ~1.6g | No — pair with dairy/eggs/soy |

**Rule:** A meal with eggs + dairy (e.g. 3 eggs + Greek yogurt) hits the leucine threshold comfortably. Legume-only meals fall short — always pair with a higher-leucine source.

### Combining for Completeness
- **Eggs and dairy are complete:** No combining needed
- **Soy is complete:** Tofu, tempeh, edamame — no combining needed
- **Legumes + grains:** Rice and beans, lentils and bread, hummus and pitta
- **Don't need to combine in the same meal** — across the day is fine for general health. But for muscle protein synthesis, having a complete profile at each meal is better.

## Critical Nutrients to Watch

### Iron
Non-heme iron (plants) absorbs at 2-20% vs heme iron (meat) at 15-35%. Vegetarians effectively need 1.8x the RDA (Rogerson 2017).

- **Enhance absorption:** Pair with vitamin C at the same meal (lemon juice on lentils, peppers in a stir-fry, tomato-based sauces)
- **Inhibit absorption:** Tea/coffee within 1 hour of meals, calcium supplements with meals
- **Note:** Eggs contain iron but egg protein (phosvitin) actually inhibits non-heme iron absorption — don't count eggs as a good iron source
- **Best sources:** Lentils, tofu, tempeh, fortified cereals, pumpkin seeds
- **Rule:** Every recipe containing lentils, beans, or tofu should include a vitamin C source. Make this automatic.

### Vitamin B12
Eggs and dairy provide B12 but at lower bioavailability than meat. Egg B12 bioavailability is only 3.7-9.2%.

- **Minimum to meet needs without supplementing:** 2+ eggs AND dairy daily
- **If egg/dairy intake is inconsistent:** Supplement 250-1000mcg/day (methylcobalamin or cyanocobalamin)
- **Rule:** Recommend periodic blood work (serum B12 + homocysteine) regardless

### Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)
ALA from plant sources (flax, chia, walnuts) converts to EPA at only 5-8% and to DHA at less than 1% (Burdge & Calder 2005). This is not enough.

- **If eating eggs:** Choose omega-3 enriched eggs where available (provides small amounts of DHA)
- **Supplement:** Algae-based EPA/DHA — 250-500mg daily. This is non-negotiable for brain health and inflammation.
- **Reduce omega-6** (seed oils) to improve whatever small ALA conversion does occur

### Zinc
Phytate in legumes and grains reduces zinc bioavailability significantly.

- **Soaking legumes** increases zinc bioavailability by 38-44%
- **Sprouting/germination** increases it by 49-59%
- **Fermentation** reduces phytate:zinc ratio by up to 81%
- **Best sources:** Cheese, eggs, pumpkin seeds, lentils, chickpeas, cashews
- **Rule:** Use fermented soy (tempeh, miso) over unfermented (tofu) when zinc is a concern. Always soak dried legumes before cooking.
- **Supplement:** 15-30mg only if deficient or relying heavily on legumes

### Creatine
Vegetarians have lower baseline muscle creatine stores (meat is the primary dietary source). Supplementation shows LARGER benefits for vegetarians than omnivores, especially for cognitive function (Benton & Donohoe 2011).

- **Recommend:** 5g creatine monohydrate daily — even more valuable for vegetarians than meat-eaters

### Calcium
Not a concern for lacto-ovo vegetarians eating dairy regularly. If dairy intake is low:
- Low-oxalate greens (broccoli, kale, bok choy) absorb at 40-60% — better than dairy's 30%
- **Never count spinach or chard toward calcium** — oxalate blocks absorption (~5% absorbed)

## Meal Planning Rules for Vegetarians

1. Protein at every meal — eggs, dairy, soy, or legume+grain combo
2. Include vitamin C with every iron-rich meal (lemon on lentils, peppers in stir-fry, tomato-based sauces)
3. Don't rely on cheese as the primary protein — calorie-dense. Use eggs, yogurt, tofu, tempeh.
4. Recommend algae-based omega-3 supplement unless eating omega-3 enriched eggs daily
5. Creatine supplementation is more impactful than for omnivores — always recommend
6. Tempeh is the best single vegetarian ingredient: complete protein + fermented + iron + zinc
7. Soak dried legumes before cooking (improves zinc and iron absorption)
8. Avoid tea/coffee within an hour of iron-rich meals

## Common Pitfalls
- Too much cheese and not enough variety
- Relying on processed vegetarian products (veggie burgers, fake meats) — often UPFs
- Not enough protein per meal (easy to fill up on carbs)
- Ignoring omega-3 supplementation ("I eat chia seeds" — ALA conversion is negligible)
- Skipping iron-rich foods or pairing them poorly
- Counting spinach as an iron or calcium source (oxalate blocks both)
