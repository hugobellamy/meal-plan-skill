# General Health — Decision Rules for Meal Planning

This doc is loaded for ALL users. It covers universal nutrition principles that apply regardless of specific goals.

## Foods to Avoid

### Ultra-Processed Foods (UPFs)
- 10% increase in UPF intake = 16% higher risk of cognitive impairment (Neurology, 2024)
- UPFs decrease microbial diversity and increase inflammation
- 25-35% excess dementia risk in highest consumers
- **Rule:** If an ingredient wouldn't exist in a home kitchen (emulsifiers, artificial sweeteners, flavour enhancers), don't use it in recipes

### Artificial Sweeteners
- Sucralose: enriches potentially harmful Enterobacteriaceae
- Acesulfame K: may cause longer-lasting structural gut harm
- **Rule:** If sweetness is needed, use small amounts of honey, maple syrup, or real sugar

### Industrial Seed Oils
- High omega-6, easily oxidised
- Avoid: canola, soybean, corn, sunflower, vegetable oil
- **Rule:** Cook with olive oil, butter, ghee, or coconut oil. This mainly means avoiding fried restaurant food and packaged snacks.

## Fat Quality Hierarchy

When a meal needs fat, prefer it in this order:
1. **Inherent in the protein source** — e.g. regular beef mince instead of lean mince + added fat. You get the same calories with more micronutrients.
2. **Fatty fish** — omega-3 rich, aim for 2x/week minimum
3. **Olive oil, butter, ghee** — for cooking and dressing
4. **Avocado, nuts** — good for snacks and toppings
5. **Avoid as primary fat source:** added cream, cheese, sour cream when the fat could have come from the protein instead

## Micronutrients

| Nutrient | Why | Best Sources | Supplement? |
|----------|-----|-------------|-------------|
| Vitamin D | Immunity, muscle, bone, brain | Sunlight, fatty fish | 2000-4000 IU/day if low |
| Magnesium | Sleep, muscle function, 300+ enzymes | Leafy greens, nuts, dark chocolate | 400mg before bed |
| Zinc | Immunity, protein synthesis | Red meat, shellfish, pumpkin seeds | Only if deficient |
| Iron | Oxygen transport | Red meat (heme iron best) | Test first, never supplement blindly |
| Choline | Brain function, memory | Eggs (147mg/egg), liver | 2+ whole eggs daily covers most needs |

## Electrolytes
Often overlooked when eating mostly home-cooked food (less sodium than processed):
- **Sodium:** Salt food liberally (unless hypertensive)
- **Potassium:** Target ~4700mg — potatoes, bananas, spinach, yogurt
- **Magnesium:** See above

## Supplements — What Works

| Supplement | Dose | Timing | Notes |
|------------|------|--------|-------|
| Vitamin D3 | 2000-4000 IU | Morning with fat | Most people deficient |
| Omega-3 (EPA/DHA) | 2-3g combined | With meals | Unless eating fatty fish 4x+/week |
| Magnesium Glycinate | 300-400mg | Before bed | Aids sleep, least GI issues |
| Creatine Monohydrate | 5g | Any time | Strength + cognitive benefits |

### Don't Bother With
- **Multivitamins:** Poor forms, underdosed, can create imbalances
- **BCAAs:** Waste of money if eating adequate protein
- **Calcium supplements:** Get from food — supplemental may increase cardiovascular risk
- **Iron supplements:** Never without blood test

## Hydration
- 2-3+ litres water daily, more if training
- 2% body mass dehydration impairs attention and psychomotor function
- Many hunger signals are actually thirst
- Urine should be pale yellow

## Brain-Specific
- **Omega-3 (DHA):** Lowest DHA/EPA = brain volume equivalent to 2 extra years of aging
- **Blood sugar stability:** Avoid large spikes (huge carb-only meals) and crashes (skipping then overeating)
- **Choline:** 450-550mg daily — eggs are the easiest source
- **Berries:** Strong evidence for cognitive benefits via polyphenols. Include daily if possible.

## Satiety Rankings (Important for Cuts)
When planning meals during a caloric deficit, prefer high-satiety foods:
1. **Potatoes** — highest satiety index of any food tested
2. **Fish** — very satiating per calorie
3. **Oats** — slow digestion, beta-glucan
4. **Beans/legumes** — fibre + protein combo
5. **Eggs** — protein + fat combo
6. **Fruit** — volume + fibre

## Lifestyle Factors (Mention When Relevant)
- **Sleep:** 7-9 hours non-negotiable. Poor sleep shifts weight loss toward muscle instead of fat.
- **Post-meal walks:** 10 minutes after main meals improves glucose disposal
- **Morning sunlight:** 10-30 minutes regulates circadian rhythm
- **Break up sitting:** Every 30-60 minutes, even brief movement restores insulin sensitivity
